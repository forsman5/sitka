# Household economy: housing (simulation side)

## Rules

- A **house** (`HEHouse`, `type_key == "house"`) is a building, not a business: no workers, recipe, stock or balance. One house shelters one household (`capacity_households = 1`).
- **Every household lives in one house**: `HEHousehold.dwelling_id` (-1 = homeless, or housing not modeled). `HESimulation._reconcile_housing(settlement_id)` is the only writer and runs at sim start, at the end of every daily tick, and right after a house is added or upgraded in place. Rules:
  - Sticky: a household keeps its house until it ceases to exist (emigration, old age, adoption).
  - A house holds `capacity_households` households; an in-place tenement upgrade adds 3 free slots to the same house.
  - Unhoused households, ascending id, take a slot in the lowest-id house with room (innermost first).
  - A household whose house is gone or over capacity is unhoused before refilling (highest ids leave first).
  - Homeless = `dwelling_id == -1`. Because new households always get higher ids, the homeless are the newest households, the same set the earlier pooled rule picked, so homeless counts and emigration are unchanged.
- Only the **government** builds houses ("Government build" menu): 15 gold from the treasury + 50 timber from the Government Warehouse. It is blocked with `"needs a government warehouse"` until a warehouse exists, then by treasury / warehouse stock. Houses are repeatable; businesses and the warehouse stay unique.
- When no house is free (`households >= capacity`) every household's monthly **birth chance is scaled by 0.2** (`HESimulation.HOUSING_SATURATED_BIRTH_SCALE`). - Households past capacity (newest ids first) are **homeless**: each loses a member with `HOMELESS_EMIGRATION_CHANCE` (0.3) per monthly emigration pass (event `emigrate`, `cause: "homeless"`).
- **Leaving home.** A grown dependent, or a worker beyond the first `HEAD_COUNT` (2: head and spouse, who never leave), only rolls its monthly leave-home chance (5-25%) when the household is over `HEHousehold.HOME_HEADCOUNT_CAP` people (4) -- the eldest goes first, and the headcount drops as each leaves -- or when the town has an empty house (`evaluate_aging`'s `free_housing`, spent one per split). Otherwise grown children stay home, so a full town does not mint homeless one-person households. A household pushed out while no house is free sends the leaver out homeless. The leaver founds their household at their real age. Housing not modeled = always free.
- **Grown children at home work.** In the weekly hiring pass (`_reconcile_employment`) a business with an opening first promotes adult dependents of its own employees' households to workers (`promote_adults`, eldest first; event `succession`, `cause: "hired"`), and only then hires unemployed households from the pool. When a worker dies of old age an adult child at home takes the place at once (`cause: "death"`). An employer that does not need them leaves them dependents (non-working, eating).
- Seeded housing comes from the starting-town spec (`data/he_presets.gd`: `housing.houses`, and `housing.tenements` that start already densified): small village 24 houses for 20 households, mining village 36 for 30, large village 56 for 48 (already past `DENSIFY_HOUSE_COUNT`, so its first housing build is a tenement upgrade, and it starts with a Government Warehouse holding timber). Without an explicit count a town gets one house per household plus 35% (`HOUSING_STARTING_HEADROOM`). A world dict without a `"dwellings"` key is unlimited (`HESettlement.housing_modeled == false`).
- **Starting towns are specs.** The setup page offers Small / Mining / Large village (`HEPresets`) and a Custom roster (household count, starting gold, house and tenement counts, Farm/Woodlot split and business checkboxes, built with `HEPresets.custom_spec` and the spec's `include` list); each is a JSON-able dictionary (households, crews, housing, treasury bonus, warehouse stock, `format` version) and `HEScenarioSeeds.build_from_spec` builds the world. This is the new-game / save format to grow. The old hand-authored scenarios and the custom roster picker live only in `scripts/sim/harness/he_fixtures.gd` as test fixtures.
- **Tenement** (`HEHouse.DENSE_TYPE_KEY`, `capacity_households = 4`, government build menu): building one **upgrades the lowest-id plain house in place** (+3 capacity, no new lot, site never moves); only when every house is already dense does it add a new lot. Pricing follows the house rate (15 gold + 50 timber) per household gained: an upgrade (+3) costs 45 gold + 150 timber (`TENEMENT_UPGRADE_*`, the option's `upgrade_cost`), a new tenement (+4) 60 gold + 200 timber (`TENEMENT_BUILD_*`, `cost`). Always read a build's price through `HESimulation.build_cost_for(settlement_id, option)`, which picks the upgrade price while a plain house is left. `get_housing_report()` gains `upgradable_houses`.
- The dashboard Build button switches from House to the tenement upgrade when `HETownLayout.prefers_densify()` (roads had to grow, a house went unplaced, or 48+ houses stand) and a plain house is left, so the town densifies instead of growing outward. Art is a placeholder (`building_tavern_blue`); footprint stays 6x6.
- Pressing Build on a goods-priced item that lacks warehouse goods (with a warehouse and enough treasury gold) sets a standing warehouse buy order for the missing goods up to the build's cost (`HESimulation.request_government_build`, state via `build_goods_order_state`: `none` / `can_order` / `ordered`). The order stays standing, so the warehouse keeps restocking for the next build. The player presses again once the goods arrive (the warehouse buys up to `WAREHOUSE_DAILY_TRADE_CAP` a day, within its wage reserve).

- **Stone house** (`HEHouse.is_stone`): an in-place upgrade or a new lot. Its household gains `STONE_MORALE_BONUS` (8%) morale and needs `STONE_HEAT_SAVING` (25%) less heat, whoever lives there: `HEHouse.heat_factor()` is copied to `HEHousehold.home_heat_factor` by `_reconcile_housing`, and `HEHousehold.need_scale(need)` applies it to the heat requirement in the purchase target, the daily use forecast, consumption and the household and person pages. About 150 g a year for four people at timber 4.0 (about 10 at 0.25). Nothing yet steers richer households into stone houses; see `household-housing-choice-plan.md` if that branch has landed.

- **Housing tab layout.** Stats on the left, the government builds on the right as one row each (button, name, icon costs, one-line state), so they are on the tab's first screen. Costs are icons (gold from the treasury, goods from the warehouse), red when the town is short of that item. The warehouse order rules (a build that lacks goods turns its button into Order, which sets a standing buy order) are explained once, on the "Government builds (?)" tooltip. Below: the house upkeep readout and three charts (repair goods used and repair visits, trailing 30 days; houses overdue and stone bonuses lost), fed by each day's `record["maintenance"][settlement_id]` (see `household-maintenance.md`).
- **Heat on the Needs page.** The heat need detail lists "Saved by stone houses" (need units the homes spared, and its share of the demand without them) and charts it as a series beside the goods that met the need and the unmet line. `get_need_detail()` returns `saved` and `saved_history`; `_consume_need` books `record["needs"][id]["saved"]`. A saving is not a good, so it is shown as another way the need was covered, not as a "met by" line.

## Where housing shows up

**Dashboard** (`he_dashboard.gd`)
- Top bar: a second line under Health/Morale, `Housing  41 houses / 30 households`, yellow with "(full)" and red with "(N homeless)". "unlimited" when housing is not modeled.
- Housing tab (after Goods): large Houses / Households / Free houses / Homeless households readouts, the rules in one line, and a "Build house" button that reuses the government build path and shows the blocker (e.g. "needs a government warehouse").
- The Government build menu also lists House.

**Town view** (`he_town_layout.gd`, `he_town_view.gd`)
- Houses are a second occupant kind. A site holds a business (`business_id`) or a house (`dwelling_id`), never both; key an occupant by `(type_key, id)`. Houses never fail, so a house site is never vacated or reused.
- `HESimulation.get_dwelling_reports(settlement_id)` returns `{dwelling_id, settlement_id, name, type_key}` per house, ascending id; it is what the layout reconciles against (`HETownLayout.reconcile(reports, dwellings)`). `get_business_reports()` never includes houses.
- Houses (`ZONE_HOUSING`, 6x6 footprints) take lots along the road frontage, nearest the civic anchor first, rotated to face the road; the site records its `road_id`. The road network (`get_roads()`: spokes from the civic anchor plus lanes) is seeded at construction and only grows: when no lot is left, `_grow_roads()` adds lanes that avoid existing sites. Town-zone businesses front the spokes out to `MAIN_STREET_RADIUS` (houses start beyond it); field and pasture businesses keep their spiral candidates but clear the roads. Footprints can now be rotated by any yaw, so `site_rect()` is the axis-aligned bounds and `site_contains()` the exact test. Houses without room are counted with unplaced businesses (`unplaced_dwelling_ids()`).
- Art: `building_home_A_blue` / `building_home_B_blue`, alternating by site id. House labels are hidden (selection shows who lives there, `HETownView.occupants_text`, e.g. "Home of household 12", plus the housing line); a housing readout sits in the view's top right (`HETownView.housing_text`).
- Harness: `check_town_layout.gd` and `check_town_view.gd` cover houses; `check_housing_assignment.gd` covers assignment.
